# TOEIC Reading (Parts 5-7): strategies, grammar taxonomy, timing and drills for a 30-day plan

Notes compiled 2026-11-21. The source mix is official format pages (IIBC, the Japanese TOEIC administrator for ETS), commercial prep sites (EN/VI) and academic SLA research. **ETS does not publish a per-category breakdown of Part 5, or a per-part timing plan.** Every percentage or minute split below comes from prep sources and should be read as an estimate. All example sentences are original. None are copied from tests.

## Official format (context for all questions)

### Takeaway
The Reading section is 100 questions in 75 minutes. Part 5 has 30 incomplete sentences. Part 6 has 16 items (4 texts x 4 questions; the options are words, phrases or whole sentences). Part 7 has 54 items: 29 single-passage plus 25 multiple-passage.

### Cited Findings
- Reading section: 75 min, 100 questions. Part 5 = 30. Part 6 = 16, four questions per text, options are "words, phrases, or a sentence". Part 7 = 54 (29 single + 25 multiple-passage). Some questions ask where a sentence best fits in a text — [IIBC official format](https://www.iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/about/format.html)
- Single passages: about 10 texts with 2-4 questions each. Multiple passages: 5 sets of 5 questions, usually 2 double sets (Q176-185) and 3 triple sets (Q186-200) — [Lumetest triple passages](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-part-7-triple-passages-3-sets-cross-referenced); [Open Exam Prep cheat sheet](https://open-exam-prep.com/cheat-sheet/toeic)
- Part 5 was cut from 40 to 30 questions in the current (2016/2018) format, so older "40-question" breakdowns need rescaling — [PREP VN](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/meo-lam-bai-thi-toeic-part-5)
- Part 7 text types include emails, ads, notices, articles, forms, tables and **text-message / online chat chains** — [ESL-Lounge Part 7](https://www.esl-lounge.com/student/toeic-reading-part-seven.php); [bestmytest VI sample](https://www.bestmytest.com/vi/toeic/toeic-reading-7/214)

### Inferences
- Part 7 is 54% of the Reading questions. A 30-day plan that drills only grammar (Part 5) caps Reading gains. Part 7 stamina and speed must get a large share of practice time.

## Part 5: categories, approximate share, quick-solve rules, collocations and confusable pairs

### Takeaway
Part 5 splits roughly into **grammar/structure items**, which can be solved from sentence structure (word form, verb form/tense/voice/agreement, preposition vs conjunction, pronoun, relative clause), and **vocabulary/collocation items**, where the four options are the same part of speech and meaning decides. Sources disagree on the split. Estimates range from about 40-50% grammar up to about 60-70% grammar. A workable planning figure is **about 50/50, with word form the single largest grammar type**. Structure items should take 5-15 seconds each.

### Cited Findings
**Share estimates (conflicting, none official)**
- Grammar is about 40-50% of Part 5. Vocabulary questions (four options, same part of speech) are about 50-60% — [Open Exam Prep grammar](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar); [Open Exam Prep vocabulary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-vocabulary)
- A Chinese-language PREP blog says grammar is about 60-70% and vocabulary about 30-40%, credited to "ETS statistics" that I could not verify — [PREP zh-hant Part 5](https://prepedu.com/zh-hant/blog/toeic-reading-part-5)
- An old-format (40-question) VN handout gives grammar (word form, verb form, relative clause, conditional, conjunction, preposition) about 23-25 items and vocabulary about 15-17, i.e. about 60/40 — [Scribd Part 5 TOEIC](https://www.scribd.com/document/521064725/Part-5-Toeic)
- ZIM says vocabulary-related items are up to about 90% of Part 5, but counts word form plus meaning items together. Definitions differ, so this is an outlier — [ZIM từ vựng Part 5](https://zim.vn/tu-vung-part-5-toeic)
- Word forms and verb tenses are the two most heavily tested grammar categories — [Open Exam Prep grammar](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar)
- In many Part 5 items the four options are four forms of one word, so position in the sentence decides the answer — [Open Exam Prep / search summary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar). Prioritize word form: it is frequent in Parts 5 and 6 and easy to learn — [VOZ thread](https://voz.vn/p/40668463/)
- Word form, preposition, connector and relative-pronoun items are decided by the sentence frame rather than meaning — [Lumetest Part 5 sentence patterns](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-part-5-sentence-patterns)

**Category taxonomy collected across sources**
1. Word form / part of speech by position
2. Verb tense (time-marker signals)
3. Verb voice (active/passive)
4. Subject-verb agreement
5. Verb form (to-V / bare V / V-ing / V3)
6. Pronouns (case, reflexive, possessive)
7. Preposition vs conjunction vs adverb (connectors)
8. Relative clauses / relative pronouns
9. Comparatives/superlatives, correlatives (both...and, either...or, not only...but also), participle adjectives (-ing vs -ed). Prep sources list these as recurring but give no frequency
10. Vocabulary: meaning, collocation, phrasal verbs, confusable words
— [PREP VN tips](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/meo-lam-bai-thi-toeic-part-5); [PREP VN ngữ pháp Part 5](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/ngu-phap-part-5-toeic); [Open Exam Prep grammar](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar)

**Quick-solve rules: word form by position** — [Open Exam Prep grammar](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar)
- Before a noun → adjective. After a linking verb (be, become, seem, remain, appear) → adjective.
- Between an auxiliary and a main verb → adverb. After a complete clause → adverb. Before an adjective → adverb.
- After an article, possessive or demonstrative → noun. After a preposition → noun or gerund.
- Suffixes. Noun: -tion, -ment, -ance/-ence, -ity, -ness, -ist, -er/-or. Verb: -ate, -ify, -ize, -en. Adjective: -ful, -less, -able/-ible, -ive, -ous, -al, -ic. Adverb: -ly.
- Original example: "The manager responded ___ to the complaint." (prompt / promptly / promptness / prompted). The clause is complete, so an adverb is needed → *promptly*. You do not need to read the rest of the sentence.

**Tense, voice and agreement signals** — [Open Exam Prep grammar](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar); [PREP VN](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/meo-lam-bai-thi-toeic-part-5)
- Present simple: usually, regularly, every month. Present progressive: currently, at the moment. Past: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2023, previously. Present perfect: since, for, already, yet, recently, so far. Past perfect: by the time / before + past clause. Future: tomorrow, soon, next week.
- Voice: if an object follows the blank → active. If no object follows (a prepositional phrase, an adverb, or the end of the sentence) → passive (be + V3).
- Agreement: ignore prepositional phrases between subject and verb. Collective nouns (management, staff, committee) take a singular verb, as do each/every/everyone/either/neither and gerund subjects.
- Verb forms: to-V after hope/choose and for purpose. Bare V after modals and make/let/have. V-ing after avoid/finish/spend (time). V3 in perfect and passive forms.

**Preposition vs conjunction rule** — [Open Exam Prep vocabulary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-vocabulary); [MyTour](https://mytour.vn/vi/blog/bai-viet/reveal-effective-and-detailed-strategies-for-toeic-part-5-exams.html)
- A noun phrase or gerund after the blank → preposition. A subject + verb after the blank → conjunction.
- Concession: despite / in spite of (prep) vs although / even though / though (conj).
- Reason: because of / due to / owing to / as a result of (prep) vs because / since / as (conj).
- Time: during / prior to / following (prep) vs while / when / after / before (conj).

**Time per item:** 15-20 s target — [Open Exam Prep grammar](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-grammar). Easy items about 10 s, at most about 30 s, then skip and return — [PREP VN](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/meo-lam-bai-thi-toeic-part-5). Skip if undecided after 20 s — [search summary of prep blogs, e.g. Engoo](https://tutor.engoo.com/blog/test-prep/5-toeic-reading-tips-for-your-students/)

**Most-tested collocations (prep-site lists)**
- Verb + noun: meet a deadline/requirement, place/fill an order, submit an application, reach an agreement, conduct a survey/research/an audit, implement a policy, launch a product, renew a contract, waive a fee, file a complaint/claim, address an issue, resolve a dispute, grant permission, exceed expectations, incur a cost, generate revenue, fill a vacancy, draw up a contract, take effect — [Lumetest collocation list](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-collocation-list); [Open Exam Prep vocabulary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-vocabulary)
- Adjective + noun: competitive price, considerable delay, prior notice, prompt payment, tentative schedule, valid ID, written consent, eligible applicant, extensive experience, outstanding balance — [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-collocation-list)
- Verb/adjective + preposition: comply with, consist of, result in/from, account for, apply for (job) / apply to (rule), subscribe to, participate in, rely/depend on, respond to, coincide with, eligible for, compatible with, subject to, responsible for — [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-collocation-list); [Open Exam Prep vocabulary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-vocabulary)
- Noun + preposition: access to, increase/decrease in, demand for, solution to, effect on, alternative to, discount on, delay in — same sources
- Verb + noun pairings are the largest collocation group. Distractors are usually near-synonyms, so meaning alone often fails (e.g. ___ a deadline: meet / reach / arrive / achieve) — [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-collocation-list)
- Phrasal verbs: call off (cancel), put off (postpone), look into (investigate), carry out, set up, run out of, take over — [Open Exam Prep vocabulary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-vocabulary)

**Confusable pairs**
- affect/effect, accept/except, assure/ensure/insure — [Open Exam Prep vocabulary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-incomplete-sentences/incomplete-sentences-vocabulary)
- advice/advise, complement/compliment, council/counsel, principal/principle, stationary/stationery, hard/hardly, historic/historical, everyday/every day, lose/loose, discrete/discreet — [PREP VN](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/meo-lam-bai-thi-toeic-part-5)
- lend/borrow cited as a frequent Part 5 confusable — [thewhyk (KR blog)](https://thewhyk.tistory.com/2)

### Inferences
- Fastest 30-day gain: master about 10 frame rules (position → part of speech, prep vs conj, voice by object, agreement, tense markers, verb forms after common verbs, relative pronoun who/which/whose/that, pronoun case). These cover the "structure" half of Part 5, which a learner can answer in under 15 s with high accuracy. Vocabulary items need cumulative collocation study, best done in parallel through daily flashcards (about 15-20 min).
- Suggested triage order inside Part 5: look at the options first. If they are four forms of one word, or a mix of preps and conjunctions, solve from the frame. If they are four different words of the same part of speech, read the full sentence and check the collocation.

### Gaps
- No official ETS frequency per category. Shares for comparatives, correlatives and participles could not be sourced. One option is to have the learner self-tag 2-3 full official ETS tests to build a personal error profile.
- "take place", "in charge of" etc. were not confirmed by a retrieved source as tested items.

## Part 6: question types and use of context

### Takeaway
There are 4 texts x 4 questions. Each text has **one sentence-insertion item**. The other three are grammar (often tense, decided by context across sentences), vocabulary, or connector/transition items. Unlike Part 5, the answer often depends on sentences outside the one with the blank. Budget about 8-10 minutes in total (about 2 min per text).

### Cited Findings
- 16 items, 4 per text. Options are words, phrases or a sentence — [IIBC](https://www.iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/about/format.html)
- One question out of the four in each passage asks for the most suitable sentence to fill a gap — [bestmytest Part 6](https://www.bestmytest.com/cn/toeic/blog-lesson/toeic-reading/master-toeic-reading-part-6)
- In insertion items all four options are usually grammatical, and the task is fit with context — [Open Exam Prep Part 6 insertion](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-text-completion/text-completion-insertion)
- Insertion technique — [Open Exam Prep Part 6 insertion](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-text-completion/text-completion-insertion):
  1. Identify the genre, purpose and tone (email, memo, notice, ad).
  2. Track pronoun references. A pronoun in the candidate sentence must have an antecedent before the gap, and pronouns after the gap must refer back to it.
  3. Use chronological markers (first/next/finally, dates).
  4. "Sandwich test": the sentence must link to the sentence before AND lead into the sentence after.
  5. Check that it does not contradict other information.
- Time: about 6-7 min for all of Part 6 per one guide. 1.5-2 min per text per another — [search summary: bestmytest](https://www.bestmytest.com/cn/toeic/blog-lesson/toeic-reading/master-toeic-reading-part-6); [PREP ID Part 6](https://prepedu.com/id/blog/toeic-reading-part-6). About 10 min, 30-40 s per item — [prep blog summary, e.g. Engoo](https://tutor.engoo.com/blog/test-prep/5-toeic-reading-tips-for-your-students/)

### Inferences
- Practical method (synthesized): read the text from the top rather than jumping between blanks, because tense and connector items depend on what came before (e.g. a letter that says the event "was held last week" makes later verbs past tense). Original example: "...Our new branch opens on 3 May. ___ , the first 100 customers will receive a gift." For the connector, ask whether the logical relation is addition, contrast, result or sequence. "In addition" or "Also" fits, "However" does not.
- Connector categories to drill: addition (in addition, furthermore, moreover), contrast (however, nevertheless, on the other hand), result (therefore, as a result, consequently), sequence/time (afterward, meanwhile, subsequently), example (for instance). These are standard categories. I found no TOEIC-specific frequency source.
- Insertion items cost the most time. Do the other 3 questions in the text first, then the insertion item, once the whole text is understood.

### Gaps
- No source gave a frequency split among grammar, vocabulary and connector items in Part 6.

## Part 7: question types, per-passage time budgets, order of attack, skimming/scanning, paraphrase

### Takeaway
The core types are main idea/purpose, detail, NOT/EXCEPT, inference, vocabulary-in-context (synonym), sentence insertion ("[1]-[4]" positions), text-message intent ("what does X mean when she writes '...'?") and cross-reference in double/triple sets. A common budget is about 55 minutes for Part 7. That is about 1 minute per question, and 5-6 minutes per 5-question multiple-passage set (about 30 min for Q176-200). Read the question stems first, scan for keywords and paraphrases, leave NOT questions and insertion items until last within a set, and never read every passage in full before looking at the questions.

### Cited Findings
**Question types and tactics**
- Detail: scan for proper nouns, numbers and dates, then read around them. Correct options paraphrase the text. Traps are options that reuse the exact words but attach them to the wrong fact, and temporal distractors — [Open Exam Prep single passages](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-single-passages/single-passages-strategies)
- NOT/EXCEPT: treat each option as true/false and verify it in the text. Often based on lists. Do these last because they take longest — [Open Exam Prep single passages](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-single-passages/single-passages-strategies); [Tiếng Anh Mỗi Ngày Part 7](https://tienganhmoingay.com/meo-thi-toeic/meo-thi-toeic-part-7/)
- Vocabulary-in-context: substitute each option into the sentence. The trap is the most common dictionary sense. Treat phrasal verbs as one unit — [Open Exam Prep single passages](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-single-passages/single-passages-strategies)
- Inference ("suggest/imply/infer/indicate"): the answer must follow from a stated fact, often by joining two clues. Avoid outside knowledge and overgeneralizing — [Open Exam Prep single passages](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-single-passages/single-passages-strategies). Answer only from the passage, even when it differs from real life — [Tiếng Anh Mỗi Ngày](https://tienganhmoingay.com/meo-thi-toeic/meo-thi-toeic-part-7/)
- Main topic/purpose and cross-passage items: in double passages at least one question needs both texts — [Tiếng Anh Mỗi Ngày](https://tienganhmoingay.com/meo-thi-toeic/meo-thi-toeic-part-7/)
- Sentence insertion ("where does the following sentence best belong?") is officially confirmed — [IIBC](https://www.iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/about/format.html). Practice sets show [1]-[4] markers in emails — [search result: VN practice doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1VJMJDbt9gQrvknMP1reztNlpbqPC66Rq/export?format=pdf)
- Triple sets: about 2-3 of the 5 questions need cross-document linking (comparing qualifications, matching a person to an event, a promise vs what was delivered, timeline). Single-document items are usually vocabulary, detail or purpose — [Lumetest triple passages](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-part-7-triple-passages-3-sets-cross-referenced)
- Non-inference questions generally follow the order of the passage — [Tiếng Anh Mỗi Ngày](https://tienganhmoingay.com/meo-thi-toeic/meo-thi-toeic-part-7/)
- Check tables, forms, footnotes and "notes", which often hold the answer — [Tiếng Anh Mỗi Ngày](https://tienganhmoingay.com/meo-thi-toeic/meo-thi-toeic-part-7/); [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-part-7-triple-passages-3-sets-cross-referenced)

**Order of attack / skimming-scanning**
- Skim the title, headers and first line, read the question stems (not the options), then scan for targets and read closely only there — [Open Exam Prep single passages](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/toeic/reading-single-passages/single-passages-strategies)
- Multi-passage sets: about 10 s per document to get its purpose (title, opening, closing/call to action). Classify each question as single-document or cross-document. Do not read every passage in full first — [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-part-7-triple-passages-3-sets-cross-referenced)
- Answer factual questions first, then inference, then NOT. Use answers already confirmed to eliminate options in NOT items — [Tiếng Anh Mỗi Ngày](https://tienganhmoingay.com/meo-thi-toeic/meo-thi-toeic-part-7/)
- It is often not necessary to read the entire passage — [Engoo blog](https://tutor.engoo.com/blog/test-prep/5-toeic-reading-tips-for-your-students/)
- Paraphrase recognition (synonyms between question and passage) is the key skill for detail items — [VnExpress mẹo Part 7](https://vnexpress.net/meo-ghi-diem-toeic-part-7-4360386.html); [MyTour paraphrasing Part 7](https://mytour.vn/vi/blog/bai-viet/huong-dan-cach-paraphrasing-trong-toeic-part-7.html)

**Time budgets**
- Part 7 about 45-55 min. One guide gives 55 min (about 1 min/question), another about 50 min — [prep blog summary: Engoo](https://tutor.engoo.com/blog/test-prep/5-toeic-reading-tips-for-your-students/); [3D Universal](https://3d-universal.com/en/reading-study-guide/toeic); [studying.jp](https://studying.jp/toeic/about-more/timeallocation.html)
- Multiple passages: about 30 min for 25 items (about 72 s/question). Triple sets 5-6 min each (the source itself states both figures) — [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-part-7-triple-passages-3-sets-cross-referenced)
- Some guides recommend giving Part 7 priority or even doing it first. Others say finish Parts 5-6 first. Targets should be fitted to your own reading speed — [search summary: Engoo / TAC / 3D Universal](https://www.tac-school.co.jp/kouza_toeic/toeic_sk_timemanagement.html)

### Inferences
- **Text-message intent items** (synthesized; no dedicated source found): the quoted line is usually a short idiomatic reply ("Sounds good", "I'm on it", "That's a relief"). Read 2-3 lines BEFORE the quote to see what it responds to, and 1-2 lines AFTER to confirm. Original example: A: "The client moved the meeting to 9 a.m." B: "Tell me about it." Its meaning here: B already knows about or sympathizes with the inconvenience, not "please explain". Ignore the literal meaning.
- **Part 7 insertion**: same sandwich and pronoun tests as Part 6. Read the candidate sentence first, note its "hooks" (this/these/such/also/however, names, numbers), and scan the [1]-[4] spots for a matching antecedent.
- **Paraphrase drill**: after each practice set, record the text-phrase → option-phrase pairs (e.g. "postponed" ↔ "delayed to a later date", "complimentary" ↔ "free of charge") in a log. This builds recognition of TOEIC paraphrase patterns quickly.
- Suggested per-passage budget (derived from 55 min / 54 Qs): 2-question single ≈ 2 min, 3-question ≈ 3 min, 4-question ≈ 4 min. Double set ≈ 5 min, triple set ≈ 6 min. Single passages (Q147-175) ≈ 25 min, multiple (Q176-200) ≈ 30 min.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source explicitly analyzed text-message intent items. ETS's format page does not list them by name (sample tests include text-message chains). The tactics above are inference.
- No source gave the frequency of each Part 7 question type.

## Evidence: timed reading / reading-rate training and explicit grammar instruction

### Takeaway
Primary studies with EFL university learners show that **timed reading produces significant reading-rate gains (about 13-27 standard words per minute) with comprehension maintained**. SLA meta-analyses find **explicit grammar instruction more effective than implicit instruction** (Norris & Ortega 2000: d ≈ 1.13 explicit vs 0.54 implicit), with critiques that outcome measures favored explicit learning. Both lines of evidence support short daily timed-reading drills plus explicit rule teaching for Part 5/6 patterns. Neither is TOEIC-specific.

### Cited Findings
- Shimono (2018), Japanese university learners: timed reading alone, and timed reading plus repeated oral reading, both improved L2 reading fluency. Gains were 13-27 standard wpm — [Shimono 2018, Reading in a Foreign Language](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item-detail/403)
- Shimono (2023), 101 lower-intermediate Japanese learners over one academic year: extensive reading, timed reading and repeated oral reading groups all made significant rate gains while comprehension held. The most wide-ranging treatment did best — [RFL item 583](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item-detail/583); [Hawaii ScholarSpace PDF](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/bitstream/10125/67392/1/33_1_10125-67392.pdf)
- Chang, 35 adult Taiwanese EFL students: the study concludes it is worthwhile including reading-rate buildup activities in L2 instruction — [CORE: Improving reading rate activities (TR and RR)](https://core.ac.uk/download/323110037.pdf)
- Chung & Nation (2006): a Korean speed-reading course increased learners' reading rates — [search summary; ERIC/CORE results](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1176224.pdf)
- Counter-evidence: in one repeated-reading study, neither treatment nor treatment length significantly affected rate, but background knowledge did — [CORE 323110174](https://core.ac.uk/download/323110174.pdf)
- Norris & Ortega (2000), 49 samples: explicit instruction is more effective than implicit (secondary report: d 1.13 for 69 explicit vs d 0.54 for 29 implicit treatments) — [Columbia SALT: Another Look at Norris and Ortega](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/SALT/article/view/1434); [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/201198976.pdf)
- Spada & Tomita (2010), 41 studies: larger effects for explicit than implicit instruction for both simple and complex features, contributing to controlled knowledge and spontaneous use — [U Toronto summary](https://www.eas.utoronto.ca/node/790)
- Goo et al. (2015 update, 34 samples, 1999-2011): explicit instruction is still more effective — [De Gruyter / Benjamins chapter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1075/sibil.48.18goo/html)
- Critique: Shin (2010) argues that primary-study data quality, coding and effect-size use compromise Norris & Ortega's conclusions. Many outcome measures favored explicit instruction — [Columbia SALT](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/SALT/article/view/1434)
- TOEIC-specific: ETS research shows repeat test-takers' scores rise on average, with larger gains on early retests (about 20,000 test-takers) — [ETS 2017 score change patterns](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2017/jyez.html). Boldt & Ross examined training type and length vs TOEIC gains — [ETS 1998 report](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/publication/1998/isuj.html); [Boldt & Ross 2005 PDF](https://group.iibc-global.org/hubfs/page/data-resources/boldt_ross2005.pdf?hsLang=ja)

### Inferences
- Explicit-instruction effects favor exactly the controlled, discrete-point knowledge that Part 5 tests. So explicit rule study plus immediate targeted practice is well matched to Part 5/6 gains within 30 days.
- A 13-27 wpm gain is meaningful. At about 150 wpm, +20 wpm is about 13% faster reading, which over 50+ minutes of Part 7 could free several minutes. This is inference: the studies ran longer than 30 days, so expect smaller gains in a 30-day window.

### Gaps
- No meta-analytic effect size for timed reading was found. No study converting study hours into TOEIC Reading points was retrieved.

## Recommended daily minutes and drill sequencing for Reading (30-day plan)

### Takeaway
No authoritative source gives a 30-day Reading minute prescription. The synthesized proposal below rests on the format weights (Part 7 = 54%), the timing consensus and the evidence above. About 60-75 minutes a day go to Reading, moving from rule acquisition (week 1) to timed part-drills (weeks 2-3) to full 75-minute simulations (week 4).

### Cited Findings
- Answer easy questions first and skip hard ones after about 30 s — [PREP VN](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/meo-lam-bai-thi-toeic-part-5)
- Set time targets according to your own reading speed — [search summary: TAC / studying.jp](https://studying.jp/toeic/about-more/timeallocation.html)
- Timed reading and rate-buildup activities improve fluency (see the evidence section) — [Shimono 2018](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item-detail/403)
- Studying low-frequency collocations gives much smaller returns than the recurring business pairings — [Lumetest](https://lumetest.com/blog/toeic/toeic-collocation-list)

### Inferences (proposed plan, to be labeled as a recommendation in the report)
- **Week 1 (days 1-7), foundations, about 60 min/day:** 20 min explicit grammar (one cluster per day: word form/position → verb tense/voice/agreement → verb forms → prep/conj/connectors → pronouns and relative clauses → comparatives/correlatives/participles → review). 20 min untimed Part 5 practice on that cluster (30 items) with error logging. 10 min collocation flashcards. 10 min timed reading (one short business text, record wpm). Day 1 or 2: one full Reading diagnostic (75 min) to set a baseline.
- **Week 2 (days 8-14), part drills, about 70 min/day:** 1 timed Part 5 set (30 items in 10-11 min) plus review. 1 Part 6 set (4 texts in 8-9 min). Part 7 single passages, 10 texts in about 25 min. Then paraphrase log and error review (15 min). Keep 10 min of flashcards.
- **Week 3 (days 15-21), multi-passage and speed, about 75 min/day:** Part 5+6 combined in 20 min. Part 7 multiple passages (5 sets in about 30 min). Focused drills on weak types (NOT, inference, insertion, text-message intent). Timed reading continues. One full Reading test in mid-week.
- **Week 4 (days 22-30), simulations, 75-90 min on test days:** a full Reading test every 2nd day under strict 75-min timing with checkpoints (0:11 / 0:20 / 0:45). Off days are for error-log review and re-doing missed items. Last 2 days: light review only.
- Error log columns: question type / why wrong (rule unknown, vocabulary unknown, misread, time) / rule or collocation to remember. Prioritize categories by points lost.

### Gaps
- No controlled study validates a specific 30-day TOEIC Reading schedule. The plan above is a synthesis and should be presented as such.
