# Feedback–review–improve loop & 30-day scheduling for TOEIC L&R (3–4 h/day)

Researched 2026-11-21. Labels used: **[SOURCED]** = stated by the cited source; **[ESTIMATE]** = researcher's arithmetic or a practitioner rule of thumb, no primary evidence; **[UNVERIFIED]** = from secondary or unofficial source, check before stating as fact.

## 1. Diagnostics: using a full mock test to find weak parts and sub-skills

### Takeaway
ETS does not publish raw-to-scaled conversion tables (each form is equated separately), so a mock score is only an estimate. The useful diagnostic is the ETS/IIBC score-band descriptors. They name concrete sub-skills (inference vs detail, paraphrase, where the information sits in the text, connecting information across texts, vocabulary difficulty, negatives/complex syntax), so per-question errors can be mapped to them.

### Cited Findings
- **Format [SOURCED]:** 2 hours in total, 45 min Listening (100 q) + 75 min Reading (100 q) — [UEL hướng dẫn dự thi TOEIC](https://www.uel.edu.vn/Resources/Docs/SubDomain/cfis/ThongBao/Huong%20dan%20du%20thi%20TOEIC%20cap%20nhatt.pdf). Each section is scored 5–495, total 10–990. "This score is not your raw score, or the number of correct answers" — [IIBC Test Results](https://iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/guide05.html).
- **Conversion tables are confidential [SOURCED]:** the ETS examinee handbook has an FAQ on why conversion tables are not disclosed to the public. Forms are equated, so the same raw score can map to different scaled scores on different forms — [ETS L&R Examinee Handbook](https://www.fr.ets.org/content/dam/ets-org/pdfs/toeic/toeic-listening-reading-test-examinee-handbook.pdf). Third-party sources say scaling uses IRT/equating — [practicetestgeeks (secondary)](https://practicetestgeeks.com/toeic-test-complete-guide/amp).
- **Unofficial conversion charts exist [UNVERIFIED]** (e.g. raw 100 → 495 per section). They are not verified against any form — [Scribd TOEIC Score Conversion Table](https://www.scribd.com/document/740047610/TOEIC-Score-Conversion-Table). Commonly circulated charts treat Listening more generously than Reading at the same raw count. That is practitioner lore, not an ETS figure.
- **Official score descriptors by band [SOURCED]** — [IIBC (Japan TOEIC operator) score descriptor table](https://www.iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/guide05/guide05_01/score_descriptor.html); ETS PDF version (Sept/Oct 2024): [ETS L&R Score Descriptors](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toeic/toeic-listening-reading-score-descriptors.pdf):
  - **Listening 495–375:** infers the gist, purpose and context of short and long talks even with indirect responses or difficult wording. Understands details including negatives, complex syntax and paraphrase. Weak only on uncommon grammar or vocabulary.
  - **Listening 370–275:** gets the gist of long talks only when it is repeated or paraphrased. Gets details only with easy/medium vocabulary, or when they are repeated at the start or end. **Weak on:** indirect responses (key to Part 2), connecting information, details without repetition, paraphrase, negatives, complex syntax.
  - **Listening 270–5:** handles photo descriptions (Part 1) and details at the start or end with exact word match. Misses the gist of short exchanges even when the language is direct. Misses mid-text, paraphrased or grammatically hard details.
  - **Reading 495–425:** connects information across a whole text and between two texts. Handles idioms, unusual meanings and close word distinctions. Weak only on very dense or difficult text.
  - **Reading 420–325:** connects information only across a small area of text. Inconsistent on difficult vocabulary and idioms. Usually cannot distinguish closely related words (key to Part 5).
  - **Reading 320–225:** answers factual questions when the text matches the question wording. **Relies on word matching**, cannot connect information beyond two sentences, does not understand complex or uncommon grammar.
  - **Reading 220–5:** cannot infer, relies on word matching, often cannot connect information even within one sentence.
- **CEFR minimums [SOURCED]:** Listening 275 = B1, 400 = B2; Reading 275 = B1, 385 = B2. ETS says these are guidelines, not cut scores — [ETS CEFR mapping](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toeic/toeic-mapping-cefr-reference.pdf).
- **Percentile [SOURCED via search summary]:** L400 is above about 63% of 2022–2024 test takers. R400 is above about 83% — [ETS percentile ranks](https://www.pt.ets.org/content/dam/ets-org/pdfs/toeic/toeic-listening-reading-percentile-rank.pdf).
- ETS also reports "Abilities Measured" (percent correct per ability) on official score reports — [ETS Score User Guide](https://ets.org/pdfs/toeic/toeic-listening-reading-score-user-guide.pdf). This is the official equivalent of sub-skill diagnosis.

### Inferences
- Diagnostic protocol [ESTIMATE]: (1) Take an official ETS-style full mock under strict timing on Day 1. (2) Record raw correct per Part (P1 6, P2 25, P3 39, P4 30, P5 30, P6 16, P7 54) and accuracy %. (3) Tag every wrong item with the descriptor dimension it tests: gist/inference vs detail; information at start/end vs mid-text; exact word match vs paraphrase; single vs multiple passages; vocabulary vs grammar. (4) Find your current band in the descriptor table. Its "weaknesses" list is the next-band target skill set.
- Use mock scores as *ranges* (±~25–50 per section) rather than point estimates, because conversions are unofficial and forms differ.
- Per-Part accuracy matters more than the total. A low Part 2 score maps to the "indirect responses" weakness. Low Part 7 multi-passage accuracy maps to "connecting information across texts".

### Gaps
- Official raw→scaled tables: not public by design. Any specific table the report gives must be labelled unofficial.
- The ETS standard error of measurement (often quoted as about ±25 per section) could not be confirmed: the ETS score-consistency page failed to load ([ETS score consistency](https://www.ets.org/toeic/research/score-consistency.html)).
- The ETS descriptor PDF could not be text-extracted. The band text above comes from IIBC's English page, which reproduces ETS descriptors.

## 2. Error log best practice, taxonomy, review cadence, redo protocols

### Takeaway
The learning-science evidence supports an error log built on retrieval: redo wrong items later, after a delay, rather than rereading explanations. Errors followed by corrective feedback are learnable, and confident errors are corrected best (hypercorrection). Practice testing and distributed practice are the highest-utility techniques. Self-explanation has moderate utility. Vietnamese prep sources stress "chữa đề" (reviewing the test) over "giải đề" (just doing tests) but give no formal taxonomy, so the taxonomy below is a proposal.

### Cited Findings
- **Error-based learning [SOURCED]:** "errorful learning followed by corrective feedback is beneficial to learning", especially when the learner strongly believed the error was correct. Metcalfe recommends low-stakes settings rather than avoiding errors at all costs — Metcalfe 2017, Annual Review of Psychology, doi 10.1146/annurev-psych-010416-044022, summarised at [Wesleyan PDF](https://wesleyan.edu/ofcd/resources/OFCD%20Resource%20Docs/Learning%20from%20Errors.pdf) and [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079415).
- **Hypercorrection [SOURCED]:** high-confidence errors are corrected more easily than low-confidence ones once feedback is given, on both immediate and delayed tests — [Hypercorrection overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercorrection_(psychology)) (secondary); [Southampton "Beyond hypercorrection" manuscript](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/412186/1/Beyond_Hypercorrection_manuscipt_R2_Final.docx). A 2021 review lists feedback timing, error type, learner awareness and motivation as moderators — [Psychon Bull Rev 2021](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13423-021-02022-8.pdf).
- **Technique utility [SOURCED]:** Dunlosky et al. 2013 (PSPI 14(1):4–58) rate practice testing and distributed practice **high**. Self-explanation, elaborative interrogation and interleaved practice are **moderate**. Rereading, highlighting and summarisation are **low** — [APS release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html); [AFT summary](https://www.aft.org/ae/fall2013/dunlosky).
- **Vietnamese prep practice [SOURCED]:** a common mistake is "chỉ tập trung giải đề mà không quan tâm chữa đề" (only doing tests without reviewing them) — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/sai-lam-thuong-gap-khi-luyen-thi-toeic-4376477.html). Recommended practices: review each error to understand the cause, and keep a notebook of grammar points with examples and new vocabulary — [mytour.vn](https://mytour.vn/vi/blog/bai-viet/examining-detailed-toeic-comprehension.html); [PREP tự học TOEIC](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/tu-hoc-toeic-tai-nha). Dictation (nghe chép) checked against the transcript is recommended for listening — [PREP](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/tu-hoc-toeic-tai-nha). Choosing practice sources with clear error analysis is said to determine results — [PREP luyện đề](https://prepedu.com/vi/blog/luyen-de-toeic).
- **Underlying descriptors [SOURCED]:** ETS bands explicitly name paraphrase recognition, word-matching reliance, mid-text information, indirect responses, negatives and complex syntax as level-separating skills — [IIBC descriptors](https://www.iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/guide05/guide05_01/score_descriptor.html). This makes them valid error categories.

### Inferences
- **Proposed taxonomy [ESTIMATE, synthesis]**, with codes for a spreadsheet:
  - V – vocabulary gap (word or collocation unknown)
  - G – grammar rule (word form, tense, preposition, conjunction, relative clause)
  - A – audio missed (did not catch a segment: speed, accent, linking, mid-text detail)
  - D – distractor trap (similar sound in P1/P2, repeated word, true-but-not-asked option)
  - P – paraphrase not recognised (answer reworded from the text/audio)
  - I – inference/connection (multi-sentence or multi-passage linking, indirect response in P2)
  - T – time ran out or guessed blind
  - C – careless (knew it; misread or mis-bubbled)
  - Q – question misread (NOT/EXCEPT, which speaker, "what will the man do next")
- **Fields to record per error [ESTIMATE]:** date; test ID/Part/question #; question type (e.g. P3 "inference/next action", P5 "word form", P7 "NOT true", "double passage"); your answer, correct answer and **confidence (sure/unsure/guess)**; error code(s); one-line root cause in your own words (self-explanation); the fix (rule, word, transcript line); redo dates; result of redo.
- **Redo protocol [ESTIMATE, from spacing and testing evidence]:** do NOT read the explanation before attempting a redo. Redo each wrong item (a) same day after reviewing, (b) +2–3 days, (c) +7 days. Retire an item after 2 consecutive correct cold redos. For listening: replay the clip → answer → shadow or dictate the key sentence → compare with the transcript. Treat "lucky guess" correct answers as errors.
- **Cadence [ESTIMATE]:** daily 15–20 min error review (today's log plus due redos). Weekly 45–60 min tally: count errors by code × Part, then pick the top 2 codes as next week's focus.

### Gaps
- No TOEIC-specific controlled study of error logs was found. Support is extrapolated from general cognitive psychology.
- No authoritative Vietnamese source gives a formal error taxonomy. The one above is a synthesis.

## 3. Progress metrics, leading vs lagging indicators, reallocation thresholds

### Takeaway
No source gives validated "if accuracy < X% then shift Y minutes" thresholds. Those are design choices and must be labelled estimates. Lagging indicator: full-mock scaled-score trend. Leading indicators: per-Part accuracy on timed drills, time per question, SRS retention, error-code counts, and redo pass rate.

### Cited Findings
- ETS official reports give "Abilities Measured" percent correct, so per-ability accuracy is an ETS-sanctioned metric — [ETS Score User Guide](https://ets.org/pdfs/toeic/toeic-listening-reading-score-user-guide.pdf).
- **Reading time budgets [SOURCED, practitioner]:** one guide gives Part 5 10 min (max 20 s/q), Part 6 10 min (max 30 s/q), Part 7 55 min (about 1 min/q). Another gives 15/10/50 — [Engoo](https://tutor.engoo.com/blog/test-prep/5-toeic-reading-tips-for-your-students/); [INSA Lyon time management PDF](https://crl.insa-lyon.fr/sites/crl/files/time_management_for_the_new_format_toeic_test.pdf); [studying.jp time allocation](https://studying.jp/toeic/about-more/timeallocation.html). One source cautions there is "no perfect time management" and that targets depend on your reading speed — [global-exam](https://global-exam.com/blog/?p=3849).
- **Score variability [SOURCED]:** repeat test-takers' scores rise on average with each retest. Gains are largest at the first retake — Wei & Low 2017, ETS RR-17-54, n≈20,000, 6 tests over 4 years — [ETS report page](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2017/jyez.html). Part of the early gain is likely familiarity, so early mock-to-mock jumps overstate learning (inference).

### Inferences
- **Metric dashboard [ESTIMATE]:**
  - Lagging: mock scaled-score estimate (L, R, total), taken weekly.
  - Leading:
    - per-Part accuracy % on timed sets
    - seconds/question for P5 (target ≤20–25 s), P6 (≤30 s) and P7 (≈60 s), and minutes left at the end of Reading (target ≥0)
    - number of items blind-guessed for lack of time
    - SRS: daily review retention % (Anki "true retention" target about 85–90%), new words/day, mature cards
    - redo pass rate (% of logged errors correct on +7-day redo)
    - error-code counts per week
- **Reallocation rules [ESTIMATE, author-proposed heuristics]:**
  - Any Part with accuracy ≥15 points below your average → move 20–30 min/day from the strongest Part to it for the next week.
  - P5 accuracy <70% → add 20 min/day grammar and word-form drills. P5 time >25 s/q → add a 30-question timed set daily.
  - Reading unfinished with >10 items blind → prioritise P7 speed drills and enforce the P5/P6 time caps before adding vocabulary.
  - P2 <70% → daily 25-item P2 set plus shadowing of question openers (Wh-/indirect responses).
  - SRS retention <80% → cut new cards per day by about 30% (review load signals overload).
  - Error code C or Q >15% of errors → strategy and attention problem: practise underline/"NOT" checks rather than content study.
  - Mock score flat for 2 consecutive weeks while leading indicators improve → keep the plan. If leading indicators are flat too → change methods (e.g. from passive listening to dictation).
- Treat a mock-to-mock change of under about 30–50 points as noise (given form differences and unofficial conversions).

### Gaps
- No empirical source for specific % thresholds or time-shift sizes. All thresholds above are heuristic.
- No source on target SRS retention for TOEIC specifically. 85–90% is a general Anki/FSRS convention (not verified here).

## 4. Realistic score gain in ~30 days / per study hour

### Takeaway
No ETS figure converts hours into points. The most-cited rule of thumb, Japanese and based on Saegusa 1985, is about 200–300 study hours per 100 points, with diminishing returns at higher levels. An old developer-era recommendation says at least 100 hours of study are needed before scores move reliably. 30 days × 3–4 h ≈ 90–120 h, which by these heuristics suggests roughly +30 to +60 points from skill growth [ESTIMATE]. Test-familiarity and strategy gains for first-time or untrained test-takers may add more.

### Cited Findings
- 200–300 hours per 100-point gain, depending on foundation — [studying.jp study hours](https://studying.jp/toeic/about-more/studyhours.html).
- Saegusa (1985): learners in courses over 200 hours gained 110 points on average. Example cited: 350 → 550 needs about 450 hours — [Bingham, Miyazaki Municipal Univ. paper](https://miyazaki-mu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1107/files/23%281%29_009_Bingham.pdf).
- Minimum of about 100 hours of study recommended by the test's developers before expecting score change — [Bingham](https://miyazaki-mu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1107/files/23%281%29_009_Bingham.pdf); a French source attributes a similar statement to ETS — [Global Exam](https://global-exam.com/blog/?p=80034) [UNVERIFIED].
- Higher starting scores need more hours per 100 points; one table puts 500–700 h per 100 points in the 700–900 range — [3D Universal](https://3d-universal.com/blogs/2025/09/toeic-score-study-hours.html) [practitioner, rough].
- ETS research (Boldt & Ross 1998) found course objective, materials and instructor qualifications were major factors in gains, not just duration — [ETS publication](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/publication/1998/isuj.html).
- Repeaters gain most at the first retest (Wei & Low 2017) — [ETS](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2017/jyez.html).

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] Points per hour ≈ 0.33–0.5 (arithmetic from 200–300 h/100 pts). For 105 h → about 35–50 points of "skill" gain.
- [ESTIMATE] Low starters (<500) with no test-strategy training can plausibly gain more (e.g. +100) because format familiarity, pacing (finishing Reading) and guessing every item give gains independent of language growth. High starters (>800) should expect smaller gains.
- Communicate expectations as ranges, and warn that score variability between forms can mask or exaggerate gains.

### Gaps
- No ETS-published hours-to-gain table. No controlled study of 30-day intensive TOEIC prep found. Vietnamese centres' "+200 in 1 month" claims are marketing and unverified (none cited).

## 5. Optimal daily structure for 3–4 h

### Takeaway
Evidence favours: spaced and retrieval-based review (high utility); focused effortful practice limited to a few hours per day (Ericsson); breaks of some kind (Pomodoro vs self-paced is mixed); sleep after vocabulary learning helps consolidation. 3–4 h/day of genuinely effortful work is at the upper limit of what deliberate-practice research considers sustainable, so the plan should mix high-effort blocks with lighter review.

### Cited Findings
- **Deliberate practice:** effortful, goal-directed activity designed for improvement, with feedback — Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer 1993. The paper reviewed studies showing "essentially no benefit from durations exceeding 4 hr per day and reduced benefits from practice exceeding 2 hr" [secondhand quote] — [Matuschak notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zEkCRJXM9NYCXxzFoDaNhL). Critique of the definition and evidence: [Macnamara & Maitra 2019](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/macnamara-maitra-2019-the-role-of-deliberate-practice-in-expert-performance-revisiting-ericsson-krampe-tesch-romer.pdf).
- **Breaks:** Biwer et al. 2023 (BJEP): fixed Pomodoro-style breaks (24 min study / 6 min break) gave mood benefits and similar task completion in less time than self-regulated breaks — [PubMed 36859717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/). Smits, Wenzel & de Bruin 2025: Pomodoro led to faster fatigue increase and motivation decrease vs self-regulated breaks, but no overall outcome difference — [PMC12292963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/). Evidence on learning outcomes is thin.
- **Spacing:** the optimal gap between study sessions rises with the retention interval. For a 1-week test, the optimal gap is about 20–40% of the delay — Cepeda et al. 2008 — [PubMed 19076480](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/); [PDF](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf).
- **Sleep:** sleep-related consolidation strengthens new word forms and integrates them with the existing lexicon — [Mirković & Gaskell 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4821602). There is no evidence that going to bed *immediately* after learning is better — [Walker et al. 2019, R Soc Open Sci](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/145355/1/rsos.181842.pdf). An alternative view is that sleep protects from interference rather than actively consolidating — [Hulme & Rodd, UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10172182/1/The%20Role%20of%20Sleep%20in%20Learning%20New%20Meanings%20for%20Familiar%20Words%20through%20Stories.pdf).
- **Technique ranking:** practice testing and distributed practice high, interleaving moderate, rereading and highlighting low — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html).

### Inferences
- **Template day, 3.5 h [ESTIMATE]**, in blocks of about 25–50 min with 5–10 min breaks (Pomodoro or self-paced; pick what you sustain):
  1. **Warm-up / SRS review, 20–25 min:** due vocabulary cards plus error-log redos due today (retrieval first, while fresh).
  2. **Deep block A (Listening), 50 min:** targeted Part drill from the weekest area, then transcript review, dictation and shadowing of missed lines.
  3. Break 10 min.
  4. **Deep block B (Reading), 50 min:** timed P5/P6 set with a strict per-item clock, then P7 passages (single → double/triple).
  5. Break 10 min.
  6. **Review & log, 30 min:** log every error with code and confidence, self-explain the root cause, add new words to SRS.
  7. **New vocabulary / grammar input, 20–30 min:** themed TOEIC word families, collocations, word forms. Best placed later in the day or evening, before sleep.
  8. (4-h days) **Light block, 30 min:** extensive listening to TOEIC-style audio or shadowing.
- Order principle: retrieval and review early; hardest, most effortful drills when alert; new material later, followed by sleep. Interleave Parts within the week rather than one Part per week.
- Time of day [ESTIMATE]: IIG afternoon sessions run 1:30–5:30 PM on weekdays (see §7). If the test slot is known, schedule mock tests at that same clock time in the final 2 weeks.
- Protect 7–9 h of sleep, especially after vocab sessions (general sleep guidance; not TOEIC-specific evidence).

### Gaps
- No TOEIC-specific evidence on session order or time-of-day effects. The template is synthesis.
- The 4-h/day deliberate-practice ceiling rests on a secondhand quote; the original pages were not verified.

## 6. 30-day plan shape, mock cadence, taper

### Takeaway
No authoritative source prescribes a specific 30-day TOEIC schedule. Recommended shape, synthesised from spacing, testing and error-learning evidence: diagnostic mock on Day 1; full mocks about weekly, rising to about 2/week in week 4; a taper in the final 2–3 days with no new material.

### Cited Findings
- Practice testing and distributed practice are the highest-utility techniques — [APS / Dunlosky 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html).
- First retests yield the largest gains, consistent with format familiarity — [Wei & Low 2017](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2017/jyez.html).
- Vietnamese prep warning against "giải đề" without "chữa đề" — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/sai-lam-thuong-gap-khi-luyen-thi-toeic-4376477.html).
- IIG requires a minimum of 5 working days between two consecutive tests by the same candidate (per a university guide) — [HAU guide](https://hau.edu.vn/data/data/hau/files/1_%20H%C6%AF%E1%BB%9ANG%20D%E1%BA%AAN%20D%E1%BB%B0%20THI%20TOEIC%20PLACEMENT.pdf) [UNVERIFIED for standard L&R].

### Inferences
- **Plan shape [ESTIMATE]:**
  - **Day 1:** full timed mock #1 (2 h), then 1.5 h review → baseline per-Part accuracy, error codes, band descriptors → choose the top 2–3 weak Parts and sub-skills.
  - **Days 2–7 (Foundation):** core vocab (SRS, about 20–30 new words/day), high-frequency P5 grammar (word form, verb tense/voice, prepositions/conjunctions, pronouns), P1/P2 traps, P3/P4 question-preview technique. Day 7: weekly error tally and half-mock (one section) or Parts drill.
  - **Day 8:** full mock #2 → reallocate per the §3 rules.
  - **Days 9–14 (Targeted skill):** about 60% of time on the weak Parts, P7 single → double/triple passages, paraphrase training (match answer options to text rewording), dictation/shadowing. Day 14: weekly review.
  - **Day 15:** full mock #3 at the real test clock time.
  - **Days 16–21 (Speed and integration):** timed sets with per-question time caps, mixed-Part interleaving, error-log redos at +7 days. Day 21: review.
  - **Days 22 & 25:** full mocks #4 and #5, each followed by full review.
  - **Days 23–26:** close the remaining top error codes, final vocabulary consolidation (no large new decks after about Day 25).
  - **Days 27–28:** a final full mock (or strategy-only half test) early on Day 27. Then light drills and review of the error log "greatest hits".
  - **Day 29 (taper):** about 1–1.5 h light review (SRS, P2 warm-up, P5 quick set). No new material and no full mock. Prepare ID and logistics, sleep early.
  - **Day 30:** test day.
- Total full mocks ≈ 5–6 (one every 4–7 days). Each mock needs a review session of about equal or longer length (2 h test + 1.5–2 h review), otherwise the mock adds little.
- Weekly loop: Mon–Sat study → Sun (or mock day +1) = tally error codes, update metrics, reallocate time.

### Gaps
- No primary evidence on the optimal number of full mocks in 30 days, or on taper length for language tests. The taper is borrowed from general exam and athletic practice.

## 7. Test-day strategy and IIG Vietnam rules

### Takeaway
There is no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a blank. Pace Reading by Part time caps (P5 about 10–15 min, P6 about 10 min, P7 about 50–55 min). At IIG Vietnam, bring the original CCCD/passport (copies and e-ID not accepted). All personal items go in lockers, you cannot leave the room before the test ends, and violations carry 2–3-year bans.

### Cited Findings
- **No penalty [SOURCED]:** "There is no penalty for wrong answers, so we recommend you answer every question, even if you are not sure of the answer" — [IIBC FAQ](https://www.iibc-global.org/english/toeic/test/lr/faq.html); ETS Global help centre says the same — [ETS Global My Score](https://www.etsglobal.org/fr/en/help-center/my-score).
- **Reading pacing [SOURCED, practitioner]:** P5 10–15 min, P6 10 min, P7 50–55 min (about 1 min per question); Parts can be done in any order. Some advise starting with P7 — [Engoo](https://tutor.engoo.com/blog/test-prep/5-toeic-reading-tips-for-your-students/); [INSA Lyon](https://crl.insa-lyon.fr/sites/crl/files/time_management_for_the_new_format_toeic_test.pdf).
- **IIG Vietnam Test Administration Regulations [SOURCED]** (published 23/04/2025) — [IIG regulations](https://iigvietnam.com/test-administration-regulations-for-english-tests-at-iig-vietnam/):
  - Read the Examinee Handbook and regulations at online.iigvietnam.com beforehand.
  - Dress politely: no shorts, sleeveless shirts or tank tops.
  - Be on time.
  - Bring an **original** valid Citizen ID or passport. Electronic versions, photocopies and notarised copies are refused, as are damaged documents. If you are ineligible for an ID card: original birth certificate plus photo ID confirmation or stamped student card.
  - An ID/registration mismatch means disqualification with no refund.
  - All personal items go to lockers. Only ID, registration form, required documents and locker key may enter the room.
  - ID checks may include thumbprint, photo and signature.
  - Enter and sit only as directed. **Do not leave the room until the test ends.**
  - Candidates with ear/nose/throat issues that cause disruptive noise may be refused entry and must reschedule.
  - Discussing or sharing test content is banned. Penalties: score cancellation and a 2-year ETS test ban in the Indochina area (3 years for serious/repeat violations), a possible permanent IIG ban, and possible prosecution.

### Inferences
- **Listening tactics [ESTIMATE, standard prep practice]:**
  - Use direction-reading time (P1/P2 instructions, P3/P4 directions) to preview questions and options.
  - P2: if you miss an item, mark your best guess instantly and refocus. Never dwell, since the next prompt starts within seconds.
  - Eliminate "same-sound/repeated-word" distractors and favour indirect but logical responses (ETS descriptors identify indirect responses as a level separator).
  - P3/P4: preview the 3 questions before each talk. Answers usually follow the order of the talk. If lost, guess and move to the next set's preview.
- **Answer sheet [ESTIMATE]:** bubble as you go in Listening (you cannot go back to the audio). In Reading, bubble per page or set, never all at the end. In the last 2–3 min, fill every remaining blank with one consistent "letter of the day" (no penalty).
- **P7 triage [ESTIMATE]:**
  - Do single passages first if they are faster for you.
  - Read questions first, then scan for the relevant part.
  - Skip and flag any question over about 1.5 min.
  - Leave the longest triple passages, or vocabulary-in-context and NOT/inference questions that need full reading, for last.
  - Hard cap: 5 min left = guess-fill.
- **Physical and mental prep [ESTIMATE]:**
  - Do 1–2 final mocks at the same clock time as the test.
  - Eat a normal meal 1–2 h before. Go to the toilet before entry, since you cannot leave the room.
  - Check the ID original the night before.
  - Warm up with 5–10 min of English audio before arriving.
  - Use a brief reset (one breath, then the next question) after a missed P2 item.
